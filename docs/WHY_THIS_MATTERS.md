# Why This Matters: Fighting Plastic Pollution with Enzymes

## The Problem

Every year, humanity produces over **350 million metric tons of plastic**. Less than 10% gets recycled. The rest accumulates in landfills, oceans, and ecosystems where it persists for centuries, breaking down into microplastics that enter our food chain.

**PET plastic** (polyethylene terephthalate) is everywhere:
- Water bottles
- Food packaging
- Clothing fibers
- Medical supplies

It's one of the most common plastics - and one of the most persistent pollutants.

## The Discovery That Changed Everything

In 2016, Japanese researchers made a remarkable discovery at a plastic recycling facility. They found a bacterium, *Ideonella sakaiensis*, that had evolved the ability to **eat plastic**.

This tiny organism produces two enzymes:
1. **PETase** - Breaks PET into smaller pieces
2. **MHETase** - Completes the breakdown into recyclable monomers

For the first time, we had proof that nature could evolve solutions to our plastic problem.

## Why Enzymes?

Traditional plastic recycling has major limitations:
- **Mechanical recycling** degrades plastic quality with each cycle
- **Chemical recycling** requires high temperatures and harsh chemicals
- **Incineration** releases CO2 and toxins

**Enzymatic degradation** offers something different:
- Works at **room temperature**
- Uses **water** as the only solvent
- Produces **pure monomers** that can make new, virgin-quality plastic
- Could enable **true circular recycling**

## The Challenge

The original PETase enzyme is slow and unstable. To make enzymatic recycling practical, we need:
- **More active enzymes** that work faster
- **More stable enzymes** that survive industrial conditions
- **Novel enzymes** with different properties

This is where our pipeline comes in.

## What This Pipeline Does

We use **artificial intelligence** (AlphaFold) to accelerate enzyme discovery:

1. **Search databases** for natural enzymes that might degrade plastic
2. **Predict their 3D structures** to understand how they work
3. **Analyze active sites** to identify promising candidates
4. **Rank candidates** so labs can prioritize what to test

By computationally screening hundreds of candidates, we help researchers focus their experimental efforts on the most promising enzymes.

## Real-World Impact

Enzymes discovered through approaches like this are already being developed:

- **Carbios** (France) has built a pilot plant using engineered PETase
- **Samsara Eco** (Australia) is developing enzymatic recycling technology
- Multiple research groups are engineering improved variants

The goal: **industrial-scale enzymatic recycling** that can process millions of tons of plastic waste.

## The Bigger Picture

PET-degrading enzymes are just the beginning. Similar approaches could help with:

- **Methane** - Enzymes that consume this potent greenhouse gas
- **PFAS** - "Forever chemicals" that persist in water
- **Agricultural waste** - Converting biomass to useful products
- **Carbon capture** - Enzymes that fix CO2

Each of these represents an opportunity to harness nature's chemistry for environmental benefit.
